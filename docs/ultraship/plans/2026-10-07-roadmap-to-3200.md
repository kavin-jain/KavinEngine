## Track B: original contribution (research)
**Gap found (2026-10-07 literature check):**
- Scaling laws are published for AlphaZero-style board-game nets (Jones 2021, arXiv 2104.03113; arXiv 2412.11979).
- Nothing comparable was found for **alpha-beta engines with small NNUE nets distilled from public data**, nor for how that scaling interacts with the **hardware a net must run on** (a microcontroller vs a desktop).
- The research dossier's weakness #1 says the same: top strength needs big hardware.

**Question:** for a fixed device and time control, which net size and how much training data maximise playing strength? Does strength follow a power law in parameters and data, and where is the optimum on a $10 microcontroller vs a laptop?

**Method (all reproducible, all from public CC0 data):**
1. Train a grid of nets:
   - hidden size ∈ {16, 32, 64, 128, 256, 512, 1024};
   - training positions ∈ {10M, 30M, 100M, 237M};
   - same schedule for every net, on the Mac GPU (~5–40 min each).
2. For each net:
   - **fixed-nodes Elo** (pure eval quality) vs a reference net, on the cloud;
   - **fixed-time Elo** on the desktop (the M3) and on the ESP32-S3. The device side is emulated by node limits at the measured device nps for each net size, then checked on the board.
3. Fit power laws: Elo vs parameters and vs data. Derive the compute-optimal net per device.
4. Deliverables:
   - a short paper-style report (PDF + repo) with plots and every PGN;
   - the engine ships the predicted-optimal net on each device, which is a direct, testable prediction.

Why it matters: it turns "#1 on ESP32" from a tuning claim into a measured design rule other embedded engines can use. It is also a clean science story for the portfolio (hypothesis → controlled experiment → model → validated prediction).

## Research brief: "loopholes" and where we can be first (2026-10-07)
**Verdict:** no "logic loophole" gives a large Elo gain. The known blind spots are real but rare, and Stockfish's own fixes for them gained 0–3 Elo each:
- fortress (Monroe 2024, arXiv 2409.12272: SF at +7 in a drawn fortress);
- the 50-move rule (SF eval damping ≈ +2.5 LTC, commit 3af083a7cd);
- zugzwang (null-move verification "does not help", Hyatt);
- stalemate in qsearch (SF fix d2d046c2a4: "does not gain elo").

The big lever is data, not search:
- Viridithas's net log measured **+16 Elo** from mixing 25–40 % game-result (WDL) labels into eval-only training.
- Our Lichess data has no results, so this needs our own self-play data.

Dead ends (measured, so skipped):
- policy net for move ordering (≈ −100);
- dual nets (SF removed, ≈ 0.5);
- adversarial policies (Go-only literature).

- **Continuation-history "sum, not average" is an ordering no-op.** Dividing all quiet scores by 3 keeps their order. The −3.2 has another cause; retry once history drives LMR or pruning.

## Known search weakness: quiet mates found late (2026-10-07)
- **Symptom.** KRRvK mate in 2 (`7k/8/8/8/8/8/R7/1R4K1 w`) is found at depth 3 with the m2 net, but at depth 9–11 with nets that score the position +1000–1500. After 1.Ra7 the same engine sees the mate at depth 2, so a pruning step in the root's subtree cuts the quiet mating line.
- **Suspects.** RFP or null-move fail-highs returning non-mate scores combined with aspiration re-searches.
- **Impact.** Slower conversion of won endgames.
- **Plan.** Instrument which rule cuts it; then guard RFP/NMP the way Stockfish does (no RFP or NMP when the eval is at a decisive level, or return (eval + beta) / 2). The unit test stays a known failure until then.
